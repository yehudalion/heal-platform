## קטע 1 — Louis Braille

**נושא:** ביוגרפיה / המצאה · **רמת קושי:** 4 · **מילים:** 325

> Louis Braille was born in 1809 in a small town near Paris, the son of a leatherworker. At the age of three, he was playing in his father's workshop when a sharp tool slipped and injured one of his eyes. An infection spread to the other eye, and by the time he was five, Louis had lost his sight completely. Despite this, his parents insisted that he attend school like any other child, and at ten he was accepted into the Royal Institute for Blind Youth in Paris, one of the first schools of its kind in the world.
>
> At the Institute, students learned to read using books printed with raised letters, a method that worked but was slow and expensive to produce, since each book had to be printed with oversized embossed type. Everything changed when a French army officer named Charles Barbier visited the school and demonstrated a system he had invented for soldiers to read messages in the dark, using patterns of raised dots. Barbier's code was based on sounds rather than letters, and it used twelve dots per symbol, which made it difficult to feel with a single fingertip. Louis, only twelve years old at the time, became fascinated with the idea and spent the next three years reworking it.
>
> By the time he was fifteen, Louis had simplified Barbier's design into a six-dot system that represented actual letters of the alphabet, small enough to be read under one finger without moving it. He published his method in 1824. However, the Institute's own director was reluctant to adopt it, and for years students were only allowed to use Braille's system informally, among themselves, while the school continued teaching the old raised-letter method in official lessons. It was not until 1854, two years after Louis Braille's death from illness, that France officially adopted his system in its schools for the blind. Today, Braille is used in nearly every language in the world.

> Most people who eat a fortune cookie after a meal at a Chinese restaurant assume it is a traditional Chinese dessert. In fact, fortune cookies are not from China at all. They were most likely invented in California in the early twentieth century, and today they are almost never found in restaurants in China.
>
> Two different bakers, working in two different cities, both claimed to have invented the fortune cookie. Makoto Hagiwara ran a Japanese tea garden in San Francisco and said he began serving the cookies with little paper messages inside around 1914. David Jung owned a noodle company in Los Angeles and said he created a similar cookie a few years later, in 1918, to encourage struggling workers with cheerful notes. Neither man ever proved his claim, and both were popular with customers who visited their businesses. Both bakers also sold their treats at fairs and expositions, which helped the cookies become known far outside their own neighborhoods.
>
> In 1983, a mock trial was even held in a San Francisco court to settle the dispute, more than sixty years after the cookies first appeared. The judge ruled in favor of Hagiwara, but the decision had no real legal power; it was only for fun. To this day, no one knows for certain who invented the fortune cookie first.

## קטע 3 — Mary Anning

**נושא:** ביוגרפיה / מדעי החיים · **רמת קושי:** 7 (קטע "קשה" בכוונה) · **מילים:** 320 · **4 פסקאות**

> Mary Anning was born in 1799 in the coastal town of Lyme Regis, England, an area now famous among geologists for its cliffs of dark, crumbling rock packed with the fossilized remains of ancient sea creatures. Her family was poor, and her father supplemented his income as a cabinetmaker by collecting and selling fossils to tourists, a trade Mary learned from him as a small child. When he died, leaving the family in debt, twelve-year-old Mary and her brother continued searching the unstable cliffs for fossils to sell, often in the aftermath of storms and landslides that exposed new specimens but also carried real risk of injury.
>
> In 1811, Mary's brother spotted a strange skull embedded in the rock; Mary spent months carefully excavating the rest of the skeleton herself. It turned out to be the first complete specimen of an ichthyosaur, a dolphin-like marine reptile that had swum the oceans during the age of the dinosaurs. Over the following decades, she went on to identify several other previously unknown species, including the first plesiosaur skeleton ever found intact, and provided crucial evidence that entire species could go extinct, an idea still debated among scientists at the time.
>
> However, Mary Anning received little of the credit or income her discoveries deserved. As a woman, she was barred from joining the Geological Society of London, the very institution whose male members regularly purchased her fossils, studied them, and published scientific papers describing her finds, often without crediting her by name. Some scientists corresponded with her privately and valued her expertise, but publicly, her contribution to the field was rarely acknowledged during her lifetime.
>
> Mary Anning died in 1847, largely unrecognized outside a small circle of collectors and scientists. It was only in the twentieth century that historians began to reassess her role, and she is now widely regarded as one of the most important figures in the early history of paleontology.

- "Invent" לא קשור להערכה מחדש של תפקיד שכבר קיים.
- "Criticize" מרמז על ביקורת שלילית, אבל בהקשר של הטקסט ה"הערכה מחדש" דווקא העלתה את מעמדה של מרי אנינג, לא הורידה אותו.
